Film and paper profiles written from the makers' data sheets
============================================================

Each .json here is written by scripts/datasheet-profiles.py from the curves traced off one official data sheet
(Kodak, Kodak Alaris, Fujifilm, Ilford/Harman, Foma), as the emulsion dataset of the Open Negative Initiative contributors records them.
Each file names its sheet in "provenance": the sheet's reference, its official URL, the SHA-256 of the PDF it was
traced from, the charts read, what is measured and what is inferred. No PDF and no image of a sheet is shipped, and
no number here comes from spektrafilm.

What the files hold:

- Measured, as printed on the sheet: a film layer's spectral sensitivity, the characteristic curves (Status M for a
  film, less its least density; Status A for a paper, its D-min included), a film's D-min
  and mid-scale neutral spectral densities, a Vision3 sheet's and a paper's cyan, magenta and yellow dye shapes (a
  paper's kept only as the Status A densities each dye reads). A paper is taken only when its sheet, or an
  older edition of it, prints its characteristic curves.
- Inferred by the script, each listed in its file: a sensitivity or a dye past the sheet's last point; a film's grey
  exposure (its ISO 5800 speed point plus the offset Kodak Gold 200's printed Log H Ref holds over its own); a
  photographic film's cyan, magenta and yellow split of its printed neutral (the Vision3 sheets' mean dyes moved and
  widened to fit it); each dye's unit (Status M or Status A approximated by normal bands); a paper's curve under its first printed point, where its sheet
  hides it under the others (their mean); a reversal film's curve past its last traced point, where its sheet hides it
  under another's stroke (that one's points: Provia 100F's green and blue under red's). The fourth, cyan-sensitive
  layer some Fujifilm sheets print is left out.

- A black and white negative's file holds one layer and one development: its sensitivity as printed (Kodak's at
  the 0.3 criterion; Ilford's and Foma's wedge-spectrogram heights read as log sensitivity, one unit of their axis a
  log; Tri-X 320's taken from Tri-X 400 on their shared sheet), its visual density curve less base and fog, smoothed
  over 0.05 log H to lift a raster trace's steps. Its development is the sheet's reference developer (D-76 where
  the sheet prints it) at the time whose ISO 6 average gradient is nearest 0.62, among the curves traced from their
  foot; its grey sits 1.01 log over the curve's ISO 6 speed point (ISO 6's speed and ISO 2720's meter), which holds
  on a relative log exposure axis as on an absolute one. Every other development the sheet prints is kept in
  "developments", on the same axis, unread. Left out: ADOX HR-50 (no log exposure scale), Fujifilm Neopan SS (its toe
  untraced), and Rollei's (no characteristic curve).
